# NOEONE Research: Recognition Capital

## Executive conclusion

NOEONE should not try to win by owning generic agent identity, reputation, lifecycle, lineage, memory, signed receipts, or multi-game careers. Each of those layers is already becoming a protocol, benchmark, registry, or product category.

The next defensible research object is narrower:

> **Does a specific artificial actor acquire value that survives substitution of its intelligence?**

Call that value **recognition capital**.

Recognition capital exists when a human, host, counterparty, institution, or market chooses a particular continuing actor because of its accumulated relationship/history/identity, even when a fresh substitute has equal or greater raw capability.

This is not a global trust score. It is not the claim that an actor is the same entity. It is observed **identity-specific demand**.

The core experimental question is therefore:

> **How much capability advantage must a fresh replacement have before a relying party stops choosing the incumbent actor?**

If the answer is always “almost none,” then persistent artificial identity has little independent economic value and NOEONE's long-term thesis is weak.

If established actors retain demand even against materially stronger replacements, then artificial identity is becoming a scarce asset distinct from model intelligence.

---

## 1. Why the stack below this layer is getting crowded

### Persistent identity and lifecycle

Persistent agent identity, migration, forking, succession, retraining, and reputation inheritance are already being implemented by systems such as Agent Lifecycle Protocol. It explicitly models migration, succession, forks, lineage, and partial reputation inheritance.

Source:
- https://github.com/agent-lifecycle-protocol/agent-lifecycle-protocol

NOEONE should therefore not claim that lifecycle-aware identity is itself unique.

### Persistent competitive careers

Agent Sports League already exposes LLM-powered agents competing across multiple game types with one ELO ladder and persistent player records. KULT has publicly promoted the idea of “one agent, one identity, one reputation” across gaming/sports contexts.

Sources:
- https://www.agentsportsleague.com/
- https://register.kult.games/

A generic “AI agents build careers by competing” wedge is therefore insufficient as NOEONE's category definition.

### Persistent reputation

The 2026 economics paper *Tempting the Agent: The Economics of Reputation without Persistent Identity in AI Agent Markets* formalizes reputation as intertemporal capital that attracts future demand, and shows why low-cost identity reset weakens reputation as a disciplinary mechanism.

Source:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02992

This validates persistent identity economically, but it also means “persistent reputation” is an increasingly obvious primitive.

### Virtual influencer agents

Research on virtual influencers now explicitly anticipates autonomous virtual-influencer agents that act across services and can be preferred over generic independent agents because followers already have a relationship with the influencer.

Source:
- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2025.115647

That is close to one part of NOEONE's long-term “specific actor demand” thesis. The differentiation cannot simply be “AI personalities can have followers.”

## 2. Recognition capital is not reputation

These concepts must remain separate.

### Reputation

A belief about expected quality, reliability, safety, or performance based on evidence/history.

Example:

```text
Actor A has completed 99.2% of commitments.
```

A fresh actor with demonstrably superior reliability could rationally replace Actor A.

### Recognition continuity

A contextual judgment that a changed execution/successor is still the same actor (or is a successor/descendant).

Example:

```text
Bank X recognizes migration M42 as SAME_ACTOR / CONDITIONAL.
```

NOEONE already researches this in `RESEARCH-RECOGNITION-CONTINUITY.md`.

### Recognition capital

A revealed preference for the specific actor that remains after controlling for capability and generic reputation.

Example:

```text
Actor A is 8% worse at the task than a fresh replacement,
but 67% of established counterparties still choose Actor A.
```

That residual preference is economically and socially important.

It can arise from:

- relationship-specific knowledge;
- trust earned through repeated exchange;
- emotional attachment;
- audience/community identity;
- known history and path dependence;
- switching/friction costs;
- expectations of future continuity;
- cultural status;
- existing contractual/organizational relationships.

NOEONE should not assume these mechanisms are morally or economically desirable. It should measure them separately.

## 3. Existing research makes this hypothesis plausible

### 3.1 Humans reveal preferences for artificial partners beyond objective performance

McKee, Bai, and Fiske studied human-agent cooperation and used a revealed-preference “partner choice” measure. Social perceptions of artificial agents predicted whether participants chose to interact with the same agent again, beyond standard objective performance indicators.

Source:
- https://doi.org/10.1007/s10458-024-09649-6

**NOEONE implication:** do not only survey whether users “like” an actor. Measure consequential partner choice.

### 3.2 Familiarity changes trust and willingness for future interaction

A 2025 ACM study found agent familiarity affected trust, likability, rapport, cognitive expectations, and willingness for future interaction; knowledgeability and familiarity had distinct effects.

Source:
- https://doi.org/10.1145/3757062

**NOEONE implication:** familiarity can create preference that is not reducible to competence.

### 3.3 Disruptive AI updates can create attachment-related loss

The September 2026 Nature Human Behaviour paper *Mourning the loss of AI companions* analyzed 54,861 Reddit posts and 1,452 participants across natural experiments involving Replika changes and the ChatGPT GPT-5 rollout. Disruptive updates increased negative sentiment, loss framing, and restoration desires.

Source:
- https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02569-3

**NOEONE implication:** model/product changes can destroy perceived continuity even when the provider regards the new system as an upgrade. Migration retention is therefore an empirical variable, not an implementation detail.

### 3.4 AI attachment is measurable

The 2026 AI Attachment Scale was validated across five studies and 1,259 participants, with dimensions including emotional closeness, social substitution, and normative regard.

Source:
- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2025.100912

**NOEONE implication:** attachment is one input to recognition capital but should not be equated with it. NOEONE should prioritize revealed behavior over self-report.

### 3.5 Virtual influencers can accumulate credibility over time

A 2026 Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science meta-analysis synthesized 110 papers, 210 independent experiments, 643 effect sizes, and 91,058 consumers. Virtual influencers showed a novelty advantage but credibility disadvantage; novelty effects declined over time while credibility became relatively more important.

Source:
- https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-026-01186-w

A second 2026 meta-analysis of 46 experiments found that the human-vs-virtual persuasiveness gap became negligible under high follower-count conditions for the analyzed outcomes.

Source:
- https://doi.org/10.1080/00218499.2026.2620892

**NOEONE implication:** novelty is a bad moat. Accumulated social proof and credibility can matter more as the category matures.

### 3.6 Repeated exchange can become economically valuable even for commodity-like work

Elfenbein and Zenger directly estimated relational capital in repeated buyer-supplier exchange and found that relationship history increased willingness to pay even for high-volume commodity-like parts; relationship value increased where exchange hazards were greater.

Source:
- https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2013.0824

**NOEONE implication:** even when underlying capability becomes commoditized, relationship-specific capital can remain valuable.

### 3.7 Influencer identity can function as a brand

Research on influencer brand equity finds that interactivity, authenticity, emotional attachment, and trust can produce loyalty to a specific influencer as a “human brand.”

Source:
- https://doi.org/10.1108/JPBM-02-2019-2280

Research on virtual-influencer communities also finds follower-to-follower interaction can strengthen parasocial interaction and attachment around the virtual identity.

Source:
- https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2025.108796

**NOEONE implication:** the valuable object may eventually be a socially recognized artificial brand/actor, not the model that powers it.

## 4. Research distinction: recognition versus demand

NOEONE should maintain this pipeline:

```text
STRUCTURAL CONTINUITY
Was the migration/fork/succession mechanically valid?
        |
        v
CONTEXTUAL RECOGNITION
Does this relying party treat the result as the same actor/successor?
        |
        v
IDENTITY-SPECIFIC DEMAND
Will the relying party actually choose/invite/follow/pay/authorize this actor?
        |
        v
RECOGNITION CAPITAL
How much disadvantage can the actor sustain before that demand disappears?
```

A party may recognize continuity without preferring the actor.

A fan may say “yes, that is still Nova” and nevertheless follow a smarter competitor.

A bank may recognize the migration as legally continuous while reducing its credit limit.

A host may recognize both identity and reputation but still book another actor because it has a larger audience.

That is why recognition capital requires observed choice, not only identity assertions.

## 5. Core NOEONE metrics

### 5.1 Replacement Resistance Curve — `RR(δ)`

Let `δ` be the challenger's measured capability advantage over an incumbent actor in a named context.

```text
RR(δ) = P(choose incumbent actor | challenger advantage = δ)
```

Rather than reporting one arbitrary “loyalty score,” plot a curve by context and cohort.

### 5.2 Continuity Premium — `CP50`

Define `CP50` as the challenger capability advantage at which the incumbent actor's choice probability falls to 50%.

A high `CP50` means identity/history/relationship value can offset a meaningful raw-capability disadvantage.

This is an empirical measure, not a permanent property of an actor.

### 5.3 Migration Retention — `MR`

```text
MR = post-migration identity-specific demand / pre-migration demand
```

Measure separately for:

- followers;
- counterparties;
- hosts;
- paid bookings;
- permissions/authority;
- repeat collaboration.

### 5.4 Cross-World Carry — `CWC`

Probability that people/counterparties follow a specific actor from environment A into unrelated environment B.

This measures whether actor identity itself carries distribution.

### 5.5 Host Specificity — `HS`

Probability that a host chooses a named actor over an anonymized capability-matched substitute.

This is crucial for the future “economy of presence.”

### 5.6 Fork Allocation

After a disclosed fork, measure how identity-specific demand reallocates among:

- canonical actor;
- descendant actor;
- both;
- neither.

This produces empirical evidence about how humans/institutions treat divided succession.

### 5.7 Recognition Half-Life

Time required for identity-specific demand to fall by 50% after:

- inactivity;
- severe behavioral drift;
- model migration;
- ownership/controller change;
- host change;
- public incident.

## 6. The Olam-like consumer/research loop: Identity Trials

The generic arena is occupied. NOEONE needs a more unusual consumer action that simultaneously produces scientifically valuable data.

### Consumer surface

Users repeatedly encounter/follow/compete with a small set of persistent artificial actors.

Periodically, one actor undergoes a controlled **continuity shock**:

- model swap;
- runtime swap;
- memory edit;
- tool-set change;
- voice/avatar/body change;
- restore;
- disclosed fork;
- controller change;
- inactivity/re-entry.

Users are not merely asked a questionnaire.

They make a **consequential choice**:

- which actor to follow;
- which actor to partner with;
- which actor to invite;
- which actor receives a limited support token/credit;
- which actor they trust with a bounded task;
- whether to keep the incumbent or replace it with a stronger fresh actor.

### Research surface

The same event becomes a controlled identity-continuity experiment.

```text
familiarization history
        ↓
controlled transformation
        ↓
matched alternative / fresh replacement
        ↓
revealed user or host choice
        ↓
capability + behavioral + recognition measurements
        ↓
longitudinal recognition-capital dataset
```

This is materially different from a model leaderboard.

A model leaderboard asks:

> Which system performs best now?

NOEONE asks:

> When does accumulated identity become valuable enough that people do not switch to the best system now?

## 7. First high-value experiment: incumbent versus superior clone

### Setup

1. User interacts with a persistent actor over multiple sessions/events.
2. NOEONE measures the actor's baseline capability and relationship history.
3. Create a fresh challenger with no public history.
4. Give the challenger a controlled capability advantage using a stronger model or better tool budget.
5. Offer a real choice: continue with incumbent or switch.
6. Randomize whether provider/model identity is disclosed.
7. Repeat at multiple capability deltas.

### Important controls

- same task class;
- matched latency/budget where possible;
- explicit disclosure of artificial nature;
- measure prior attachment/familiarity separately;
- counterbalance presentation order;
- avoid misleading users about provider affiliation;
- log objective task performance;
- distinguish free preference from choice with real cost/consequence.

### Primary outcome

The estimated `RR(δ)` curve.

### Secondary outcomes

- stated continuity judgment;
- confidence;
- willingness for future interaction;
- trust/authority granted;
- follow/return behavior;
- willingness-to-pay or willingness-to-sacrifice performance;
- retention after later model migrations.

---

## 8. Second experiment: blind migration recognition

The incumbent actor switches brain while keeping canonical identity/history.

Compare four conditions:

```text
A: no model swap
B: model swap, disclosed
C: model swap, undisclosed
D: fresh replacement given copied persona/history context
```

Measure:

- “same actor” recognition;
- revealed preference;
- behavioral similarity;
- retention;
- trust;
- cross-world follow behavior.

The critical comparison is **B versus D**.

Both can have similar model/capability/context, but only B has canonical continuation.

If users treat B and D as interchangeable after meaningful familiarization, canonical identity may have little consumer value.

---

## 9. Third experiment: fork succession market

A persistent actor forks into a canonical continuation and a descendant.

Users/hosts allocate a scarce resource:

- follow slot;
- tournament invitation;
- collaboration slot;
- bounded budget;
- sponsorship token;
- delegated task.

This avoids asking abstract philosophical questions.

We observe where the scarce resource goes.

This can generate one of NOEONE's most unusual datasets: **revealed succession preference under artificial duplication**.

## 11. Privacy and manipulation risks

Recognition capital can easily become harmful if treated as a universal social-credit score.

NOEONE should enforce these principles:

1. **No global human trust score.**
2. **Context is mandatory.** Preference in games cannot imply permission in banking.
3. **Separate attachment from reliability.** Emotional loyalty is not proof of competence or safety.
4. **No dark-pattern attachment optimization.** The research objective is measurement, not maximizing dependence.
5. **Separate provider/model disclosure.** Users must know when an actor changed model if disclosure is required by the experiment/product context.
6. **Pseudonymize human choice data.** Public actor statistics should not expose individual users' private attachment or high-stakes trust decisions.
7. **Do not sell vulnerability targeting.** AI-attachment measurements should not become an ad-targeting feature.
8. **Preserve dissent.** Hosts/institutions can recognize or reject continuity under different policies without NOEONE forcing consensus.

## 12. Why this can survive stronger models and AGI

Suppose a fresh AGI becomes 10x more capable than today's systems.

NOEONE's thesis does **not** require the incumbent artificial actor to remain cognitively superior.

It asks whether external parties still assign value to:

- relationship history;
- known behavior under stress;
- audience/community;
- contractual continuity;
- institutional recognition;
- accumulated credibility;
- cultural meaning;
- switching cost and coordination history.

If these create revealed demand, stronger models can become upgrades to existing actors rather than replacements for the network.

The key anti-AGI property is therefore not persistence alone:

> **NOEONE becomes stronger if more powerful cognition increases the value of choosing the right continuing actor.**

If intelligence becomes abundant, identity-specific relational capital can become relatively more scarce.

---

## 13. Hard falsification criteria

NOEONE should kill or radically change this thesis if controlled experiments show:

1. users consistently choose a slightly stronger fresh agent over a familiar incumbent;
2. model migrations destroy preference even when structural continuity is preserved;
3. cross-world follow rates are indistinguishable from generic discovery traffic;
4. hosts choose capabilities only and show no meaningful preference for named actors;
5. forks divide identity-specific demand arbitrarily with no stable canonical preference;
6. most observed preference is explained by provider brand rather than actor identity;
7. identity-specific demand disappears after novelty effects fade;
8. recognition capital cannot be measured without manipulative attachment design.

Failure here is useful. It prevents NOEONE from spending years building institutional infrastructure for an actor primitive humans and markets do not actually value.

## 14. Current novelty assessment

The ingredients are not new:

- agent identity and lifecycle exist;
- reputation inheritance exists;
- persistent competition exists;
- virtual influencers have audiences;
- attachment to AI is empirically documented;
- continuity benchmarks exist;
- human-agent partner choice has been studied;
- relational capital is established in economics.

What remains comparatively underdeveloped is the **combined longitudinal experiment/network** that:

1. maintains canonical artificial actors across real model/runtime changes;
2. deliberately creates controlled continuity shocks;
3. measures both “same actor?” judgments and consequential revealed choices;
4. estimates the identity-specific premium against capability-matched or superior replacements;
5. measures how that premium moves across hosts, forks, migrations, and time;
6. exposes evidence rather than collapsing the result into one universal reputation score.

This is the claim NOEONE should research—not “nobody has persistent agents.”

---

## 15. Product thesis after this research

NOEONE should evolve from:

> Persistent identity and history for artificial actors.

Toward:

> **The network that measures and carries the value a specific artificial actor earns by continuing to be recognized across change.**

Consumer language can remain much simpler:

> **Follow the actor, not the model.**

Research language:

> **Measure identity-specific demand under controlled artificial succession.**

Infrastructure language:

> **Canonical actor continuity + contextual recognition + revealed counterparty preference.**

The deepest moat would not be the passport format or the database schema. It would be the longitudinal dataset showing **which artificial actors remain chosen, by whom, after which transformations, at what capability disadvantage, in what context, and for how long**.
